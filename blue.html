Blue — the Digital Release Manager

An AI colleague for release and delivery managers. Blue does the routine work of enterprise release management (watching readiness, chasing owners, writing reports, scoring change risk, preparing Go/No-Go packs) and reports to a human. People make every decision that changes a release; Blue reads, talks and proposes.

Built by Sujoy G.
Works with Jira · Polarion · Microsoft Teams
Status v0.7.0 · in progress
01Why I built it

Much of a release manager's week goes on work that matters but doesn't need judgement: checking which tickets are still open before a cutover, chasing owners, assembling the weekly status report, pulling together the evidence for a Go/No-Go call. The judgement itself (is this release safe to ship?) is the part that should stay with a person.

Blue splits the job along that line. It takes the routine work and does it every day, consistently, and leaves every decision that changes the state of a release with a human. It is the next step after ERSA: from one risk-scoring agent to a full digital colleague with its own governance.

02What Blue does
  • Daily readiness scan: checks every active release each weekday morning and raises a gap only when something is new or has got worse, so alerts stay worth reading.
  • Owner chasing: sends a polite, specific nudge to the owner of an item that's blocking a release, and tracks how long it takes to resolve.
  • Weekly status report: writes the report with an executive summary, traffic lights, risks and next steps, and produces it as a PDF.
  • Change-risk pre-assessment: scores each change before the Change Advisory Board meets and suggests a recommendation for the board to consider.
  • Go/No-Go pack: assembles the evidence for the release decision. Once a person decides, Blue proposes writing the decision back to the Jira release, and that change also waits for a human to approve it.
03Humans decide, Blue proposes

Every action Blue takes is checked against four autonomy tiers before it runs:

  • Tier 0, read: looking at releases, issues and risk. Always allowed.
  • Tier 1, communicate: posts, drafts and comments. Allowed automatically, within rate limits, and can be stopped at any time with a global or per-release kill switch.
  • Tier 2, change state: transitions, field edits, approvals. Never done on its own. Blue shows the exact change it wants to make, and it happens only after a person presses Approve. Unanswered requests expire after 24 hours.
  • Tier 3, forbidden: deletes, permission changes and deployments. Blocked outright, as is any action Blue doesn't recognise.

Blue reaches Jira and Polarion only through my Release Governance MCP server, so every request is already limited to approved projects and tools before Blue's own rules even apply.

04Audit, privacy and control
  • Append-only audit log: every action, including every refusal, is recorded with Blue's stated reasoning. A proposed change, the human decision and the applied change form one unbroken history.
  • Blue is change-controlled too: its instructions, rules and model are versioned together as an "agent release", and everything Blue produces names the version that produced it.
  • Evaluates releases, never people: the only personal data kept is a display name for addressing someone, and names are replaced after 90 days. A plain-language note explains all of this for the works council (Betriebsrat).
  • EU data residency: it runs on EU-located infrastructure, with storage pinned to the EU.
  • Admin dashboard: releases overview, pending approvals, audit log, KPIs, kill switches, routine schedule and alerts in one place. Blue measures its own value too: how early it spots gaps, how fast chases resolve, how often its alerts are false alarms, and how often its proposed changes are approved.
